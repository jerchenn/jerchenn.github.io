11Asking

Three approaches (third is under-utilised)

I

Start from a problem

Depart from some societal issue, broadly interpreted, that interests you and that you think is amenable to your skill set.

Risk: loving the subject, not the question

II

Start from a literature

Find one or two articles that you really like, as close as possible to what you want to do. In what ways can you add to this literature?

Risk: inheriting question and blind spots

III

Start from a method or a dataset

Begin with a skill worth acquiring, or access already in hand. Then find the question that deserves it. Underused here.

Risk: a technique in search of a purpose

12Asking

Answering one fundamental question

What causes Y?

A research area

Does X cause Y?

A research question

Sharpness
1Good research questions are sharp. They guide the structure of the dissertation, and push you to think in a stringent, theoretically informed way.
2Do not underestimate the power of such sharpness. Questions of this type enforce delineation.
3The best dissertations ask one, and exactly one, research question. The common failure I see in marking is four half-answered questions in twelve thousand words.

14Designing

The right method lights up the way

Set out the process before starting

How will information be captured and cleaned? How will it be analysed? How will the results be interpreted?

Then attack it before anyone else does

What could go wrong in this process? What is the answer when it does? Can causality be reached at all, and if so how?

In social sciences, we almost never have an experiment.

So the honest question is not whether the design is perfect. It is what can be claimed given the design in hand, and whether the text says so rather than hoping nobody asks.

18Believing

Dataset arrives already shaped

Choices made before the data arrived

Who collected the data and for what purpose? From a survey, and analysed for selection bias? Who has the rights over it? Can we gain access, and how long?

All data must be treated.

Choices still to be made

Which data do we include, and which outliers? Which variables do we include? Which questions do we ask interview subjects? Which results do we actually present?

Countless choices and tradeoffs.

Selective use can appear as deliberate falsification. Document your steps, argue for your chosen path, and be transparent.

19Believing

Published does not mean true

Publication bias

The fact that journals like to publish interesting results creates a publication bias.

A biased sample of the research done.

Selective reporting

The prevalence of the replication crisis probably means that at the very least, selective publication has been common.

Only the specifications that worked.

p-hacking

Masicampo and Lalande (2012) found that p-values just under .05 are overrepresented in all top psychology journals.

Strong incidental evidence that p-hacking is common.

Be careful even when reading published work. This is not a reason for cynicism, and it is certainly not a reason to dismiss an unwelcome finding. It is a reason to ask of every paper cited: what would I need to see to believe this?

20Believing

Citation as a record of reading

PracticeCaution
Only refer to things that you have read. If Jones (2019) says that Marshall (1890) writes at length about knowledge spillovers, go to the original source.Never go in search of references after the claim. The argument should come from the reading.
This advice goes for the classics too. If Jones says Marshall was the first to talk about the urban wage premium, try to assess that statement.If you cite something as consensus, it had better be. One paper in agreement is not a field in agreement.
Accurately represent the view of the cited work. Including where it is more equivocal than you would like.Do not overcite or build reference walls. Six citations after a sentence usually means none was load-bearing.

Generally, do not cite common knowledge, but this discussion is difficult. In this department it differs between a lawyer, an economist and a planner.

21Believing

Reference management with Zotero

I

The Word plug-in

Zotero ships with a plug-in for Microsoft Word. It adds references directly to a paper as it is being written. Add/Edit Citation starts one.

II

The browser connector

The connector for Chrome or Firefox also installs a Zotero menu for Google Docs, so citations can be added there while writing/brainstorming.

III

A record at the point of reading

The reference is captured when the work is read. It does not make it acceptable to cite work that has not been read.

23Integrity

Permitted and prohibited AI use

Five minutes to read the latest Departmental AI policy, what is your key takeaway?

I

The rule

“A student using any unacknowledged content generated by artificial intelligence within a summative assessment as though it is their own work constitutes academic misconduct, unless explicitly stated otherwise in the assessment brief.”

II

The guide

Thirty activities with guidance. Pending committee approval, so it may change. Use of GenAI without declaration is not permitted. Many uses are permitted with declaration where work is submitted for examination or feedback.

III

Prohibited in the guide

The policy's prohibited list includes asking a tool to reword your own text, and translating your own writing. Generating text is prohibited unless an assessment explicitly asks for it. So are interrogating your ideas, expanding your reasoning, and interpreting images.

If unsure whether a tool or application is allowed, ask the Student Business Office, pgadmin@landecon.cam.ac.uk

24Integrity

Declaring AI use is essential

A declaration is required where work is submitted for examination or feedback.

I

This deck

Prepared with AI assistance: slide layout, migration from last year's version, and checking of information against the handbook, the DRDS schedule and the departmental AI policy.

Voluntary: the policy's rule applies to submitted work.

II

Contents of a declaration

The tool used, the activity it was used for as listed in the policy's table, what it produced, and what was done with the output. Declared at submission in the form the assessment specifies.

Use of GenAI without declaration is not permitted.

III

Limits of a declaration

Declaration applies to permitted activities. It does not lift a prohibition. Rewording your own text, translating it, and generating text remain prohibited whether or not they are declared.

In doubt, the fundamental rule takes precedence.

25Integrity

Ethics review (more on this next week)

Ask early, even if the answer is no

Consider ethical issues before you start. Human subjects, personal data, fieldwork. Consult your supervisor.

Where to look

See the flowchart at: cshss.cam.ac.uk · ethicsflowchart_0.pdf. A Research Ethics Committee answers questions. cshss.cam.ac.uk/research-ethics-approval

Your dissertation will be part of our dissertation library. That means it will be around for a long time.

Some scientific discoveries present moral dilemmas. That does not mean we should shy away from controversy, but that we have to be careful, stringent and thoughtful.

The built environment, time use, and subjective wellbeing

I am an economist interested in spatial inequality and urban planning. I hold an Isaac Newton Trust Fellowship in the Department of Land Economy, University of Cambridge, where I am a Senior Teaching Associate.

My research asks how the places people live in, and how they spend their time, shape how they feel. I work with longitudinal survey, time-use, and health-record data, using causal inference and machine learning.

Research

Causal inference

Does where you live affect how you feel?

Staggered difference-in-differences, synthetic control, and causal forests applied to Understanding Society panel data to estimate the effect of built-environment change on wellbeing.

Paper

Planning and AI

Evidence platforms for local planning

Principal Investigator on two ai@cam projects: PLATO, a planning-evidence platform built with Greater Cambridge Shared Planning, and a social-housing allocation dashboard with Camden Council. PLATO is open to planners and researchers with an access code, available on request.
